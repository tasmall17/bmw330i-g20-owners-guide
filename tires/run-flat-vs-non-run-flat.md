# Run-flat vs. regular tires on the G20

**Applies to:** G20 330i / 330i xDrive / M340i (factory run-flat, option `258`)

> **TL;DR / ELI5:** Run-flats have super-stiff sidewalls so you can keep driving ~50 miles at 50
> mph after a puncture. The car has **no spare** because of them. The price of that stiffness:
> a harsher, louder ride, duller steering, higher cost, and usually shorter life. **Switching to
> regular ("non-run-flat") tires is the single biggest comfort-and-feel upgrade owners report**,
> and it usually *saves* money. You just need a backup plan for flats: plug kit + small 12V
> compressor in the trunk, plus roadside assistance.

## How much it actually matters: **4/5** (for how the car feels and costs)
It's the biggest lever on ride, noise, and steering after the tire category itself.

## What owners report (Bimmerpost G20 forum, many threads)
- Non-run-flats are **"night and day"**: very noticeably quieter, steering feels better
  (DWS06 Plus replacing OE run-flats).
- Bumps much less noticeable; turn-in and grip more confidence-inspiring (PS4S non-RFT).
- A dissenting data point: one owner going PZ4 run-flat → PS4S felt only a "very minor" comfort gain
  but much better grip. Another rated the stock PZ4 RFT steering highly.
- Going from 19" to 18" helps too, but **run-flat → regular matters more**. 18" non-run-flat is the
  comfort maximum.
- Consumer Reports' (older) owner data: lower satisfaction and shorter tread life for run-flats.

## Cost per tire (Oct 2026, national retail)
| Size | Run-flat all-season (Pirelli P7 AS RFT ★) | Regular all-season (e.g. Conti DWS06 Plus / Pirelli P Zero AS Plus 3) | Run-flat summer (P Zero PZ4 RFT ★) | Regular summer |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 225/45R18 | $253–312 | ~$200–216 (pick a **95 XL**) | — | $245–282 |
| 225/40R19 | $343–390 | ~$234 | $352–384 | ~$276–329 |
| 255/35R19 | $405–503 | ~$236–259 | $299–390 | ~$278–345 |

Plus many shops add **$5–15/tire** to mount run-flats. Over a set, regular tires usually save
**$150–1,000**.

## Weight (minor: sidewall stiffness matters more than mass)
225/45R18: P7 AS RFT 26 lb vs P Zero AS+3 24 lb / DWS06+ 23 lb. 255/35R19: P7 AS RFT 28 lb vs
DWS06+ 25 lb.

## What you give up, and the fix
| You lose | Fix |
|---|---|
| Drive-on-flat (50 mi @ 50 mph) | **Plug kit + 12V compressor** (~$40–80) handles most tread punctures in 15 min |
| Car has no spare | Optional "Modern Spare" style compact-spare kit (135/70R18, ~half the trunk well). **Doesn't fit M Sport/performance brakes** |
| Sidewall punctures can't be plugged | Roadside assistance (insurance, AAA). BMW's own roadside plan ends with the new-car warranty ⚠️ check yours |

## Things that don't change
- **TPMS:** same sensors work. In iDrive: Vehicle status → Tire Pressure Monitor → confirm tire
  settings → **Perform reset**, then drive.
- **Warranty:** non-run-flats don't void anything.
- No BMW statement found that the suspension "needs" run-flats. Owner reports are overwhelmingly
  positive after switching.

## Who should keep run-flats
- You drive long stretches with no cell signal or roadside help, or at night alone and that's a
  deal-breaker.
- You will not deal with a plug kit, ever.
- You want the exact factory feel and BMW ★ approval.
Otherwise the forum consensus is clear: **go regular.**
