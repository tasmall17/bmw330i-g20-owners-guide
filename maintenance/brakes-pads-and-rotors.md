# Brake pads, rotors & wear sensors

**Applies to:** 2019–2024 G20 330i & 330i xDrive (B46/B48), standard and M Sport brakes · **Category:** maintenance

> **TL;DR / ELI5:** Pads are the friction blocks that squeeze the discs (rotors) to stop the car. Both
> wear out. BMW puts a little wire sensor in one pad per axle that triggers a dashboard warning when the
> pads are thin, usually with ~2,000 miles left. On BMWs the rotors are usually worn to their limit by the
> time the pads are, so **plan on pads + rotors + sensor together**, one axle at a time. On the G20 the
> **rears often wear out before the fronts.** Bottom line: replace when the car (or a measured inspection)
> says so, not on a schedule. An independent shop or a capable DIYer saves several hundred dollars per axle.

## How much it actually matters: **4/5**
It's the primary safety system, and running pads past the sensor scores the rotors and can damage calipers.
It isn't 5/5 only because the car warns you well in advance.

## What the forums say
- **"Measure first" camp (DIY):** reuse rotors that are above minimum thickness and run true, especially
  rears; replace a sensor only if it has triggered or is damaged. *(Common DIY practice ⚠️ unverified forum citation)*
- **"Always rotors with pads" camp (dealers, BMW norm):** BMW rotors are near minimum by the time pads are done;
  machining rarely leaves enough material; a fresh sensor every time.
- **Where consensus lands:** expect rotors with every pad change on a BMW. "Every other pad change" only works
  if a micrometer says so. Rear pads on the G20 commonly wear faster than fronts.

## When to do it
| Source | Interval |
|---|---|
| BMW (CBS) | Condition-based. Pad-wear item appears in CBS when ~2,000 mi or less remain |
| Enthusiast / forum consensus | When the sensor triggers or measured pad thickness is ~3 mm; rotors at/below the minimum stamped on the rotor |
| Signs it's due now | Brake warning / CBS pad message, grinding, pulsation through the pedal or wheel, deep lip on the rotor edge |

## DIY verdict: **Doable with tools**
- **You need:** a jack + stands (use the jack pads), large Torx/hex bits, a torque wrench, brake cleaner,
  caliper piston tool, and a **scan tool/app** (BimmerLink + BMW-compatible adapter, Carly, ISTA, Foxwell).
  New wear sensor if the old one triggered.
- **The gotcha:** the **rear electronic parking brake (EPB) must be put in service/workshop mode with a scan
  tool** before pushing the rear pistons back. Never force them by hand. Also reset the brake-pad CBS
  counters afterward and bed in the pads. Torque values and one-time-use bolts: check BMW repair info ⚠️ unverified.

### Standard vs M Sport brakes: know which you have
| | Standard | M Sport (option `2NH`) |
|---|---|---|
| Front | 330×24 mm rotor, single-piston sliding caliper | 348×36 mm, **4-piston fixed** caliper |
| Rear | 330×20 mm | 345×24 mm |
| How to tell | Caliper color does **not** tell you. Measure center cap → rotor edge: > ~7" ≈ M Sport; or check the build sheet ([`../vehicle/decode-your-vin.md`](../vehicle/decode-your-vin.md)) |
| Parts cost | Baseline | Much higher (front pads ≈ $120 pre-3/2022 build vs ≈ $332 from 3/2022) |

## Watch out for
- **"Brake system service" / caliper "clean and lube" sold as a separate line** = mostly upsell. It's part of
  any proper pad job. The only standalone brake maintenance BMW lists is **brake fluid**
  ([`brake-fluid.md`](brake-fluid.md)).
- **Ask for measured pad thickness in mm** (and rotor thickness vs. minimum) before approving.
- **Red-flag threshold:** over **$1,200 per axle** for pads + rotors + sensor on standard brakes.
- Brakes are wear items. Not covered by the new-car or emissions warranties.
