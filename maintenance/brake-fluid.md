# Brake fluid flush

**Applies to:** 2019–2024 G20 330i & 330i xDrive · **Category:** maintenance

> **TL;DR / ELI5:** Brake fluid is the liquid that turns your foot pressure into clamping force at the
> wheels. It slowly soaks up water from the air, even sitting in the garage. Wet fluid boils at a
> lower temperature (brakes can go soft on a long downhill) and quietly corrodes the expensive ABS/DSC
> pump-and-valve block. That's why BMW schedules it **by time, not miles**. Your mileage doesn't
> matter here. **Bottom line: do it when the car asks (first at 3 years, then every 2). It's a real
> service, not an upsell. Just don't overpay for it.**

## How much it actually matters: **4/5**
Skipping it risks the ABS/DSC hydraulic unit (a four-figure part) and hard-to-diagnose brake fade.

## What the forums say
- **"Stretch it" camp:** Bimmerfest threads ("Brake Fluid Flush every 2 years, REALLY?") and a G42
  Bimmerpost thread. Owners test with $10–25 moisture pens, see low readings at 2 years, and stretch it.
- **Counterpoint:** those pens read the reservoir, where the *freshest* fluid sits. The oldest,
  wettest fluid is down in the calipers and ABS unit (⚠️ unverified general knowledge, widely repeated).
- **"Do it on time" camp:** fluid is hygroscopic; BimmerLife calls the flush "cheap insurance."
  Bimmerpost notes BMW does the first change at 3 years, then every 2.
- **Where consensus lands:** follow BMW's schedule. Even skeptics rarely go past 3 years.

## When to do it
| Source | Interval |
|---|---|
| BMW (CBS, time-based) | First at **3 years**, then **every 2 years** (reported; CBS shows it) |
| Enthusiast / forum consensus | Same; track-day drivers do it before events |
| Signs it's due now | CBS "Brake fluid" due/overdue; soft or long pedal; fluid dark in the reservoir |

## DIY verdict: **Doable with tools**
- **You need:** **DOT 4 LV** (low viscosity), BMW **81 22 0 142 156**, ~1–1.5 L; a pressure bleeder
  (keep it under ~0.5 bar), 11 mm wrench, clear hose, catch bottle, and a scan app to reset the CBS
  item. Specs: [`../vehicle/specs-and-fluids.md`](../vehicle/specs-and-fluids.md).
- **Bleed order:** right rear → left rear → right front → left front.
- **The gotcha:** a screen in the reservoir stops you from turkey-basting the old fluid out first. A
  dealer can cycle the ABS pump with ISTA; a home flush gets most of the old fluid without it. ISTA
  ABS cycling is only *required* if air got into the hydraulic unit (e.g. reservoir ran dry).
- Never let the reservoir run dry during the bleed.

## Watch out for
- A separate **"brake system service" / caliper "clean & lube"** on top of the flush is mostly
  fluff. That's part of any proper pad job. See [`../where-to-service/upsells.md`](../where-to-service/upsells.md).
- Make sure it's a **full flush at all four wheels**, not a reservoir top-off.
- Use DOT 4 **LV** specifically, not generic DOT 3/4.
