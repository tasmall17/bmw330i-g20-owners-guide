# Spark plugs (and ignition coils)

**Applies to:** 2019–2024 G20 330i & 330i xDrive (B46/B48) · **Category:** maintenance

> **TL;DR / ELI5:** Spark plugs make the spark that lights the fuel in each of the four cylinders.
> Over tens of thousands of miles the tips wear and the spark gets weaker. Worn plugs cause
> **misfires** (stumbling, rough idle, check-engine light), and misfires can overwork the ignition
> coils and damage the catalytic converter. BMW calls for new plugs about every **60,000 miles**. The
> coils that sit on top of them don't need replacing on a schedule; swap one only if it fails.
> **Bottom line: do plugs around 60k on a stock car. It's a 1-hour DIY with one special socket, and
> roughly $200–330 at an independent shop.**

## How much it actually matters: **3/5** (plugs) · **2/5** (preventive coils)
Worn plugs cause misfires that stress coils and can hurt the catalytic converter. A failed coil gives a
clear misfire code and is a cheap, quick fix, not a cascade failure.

## What the forums say
- **Plugs, "BMW's interval is fine" camp:** for a stock 330i, ~60k is fine.
- **Plugs, "sooner" camp:** Euro Premium Parts (a parts seller) says every 40–50k. Bimmertalk (low
  credibility) says ~60k stock, **25–45k if tuned** (JB4/MHD), with a tighter gap.
- **Coils, "replace with plugs" camp:** you're already in there, so do coils at plug time or ~100k.
- **Coils, "only on failure" camp:** coils and plugs wear on different timelines. A misfire is easy to
  diagnose by swapping a coil to another cylinder and seeing if the misfire follows it.
- **Where consensus lands:** plugs at ~60k stock (sooner if tuned or misfiring); coils only when one
  fails. Keep a spare coil if you want insurance.

## When to do it
| Source | Interval |
|---|---|
| BMW (CBS / 2021 booklet) | Every **6th oil service** (~60k mi) |
| Enthusiast / forum consensus | ~60k stock; **25–40k if tuned** |
| Signs it's due now | CBS due; misfire codes; rough idle or stumble under load |

## DIY verdict: **Doable with tools**
- **You need:** 4 × plug **12 12 2 455 258** (NGK, pre-gapped; NGK retail cross-ref ⚠️ 96206, some
  say 95248), **14 mm thin-wall 12-point plug socket** + extension, torque wrench (**23 Nm**), compressed
  air. Specs: [`../vehicle/specs-and-fluids.md`](../vehicle/specs-and-fluids.md).
- **Steps:** cold engine → lift off the engine cover → unclip coil connectors (tabs are brittle) →
  pull coils → **blow out the plug wells** → swap plugs → torque → reassemble.
- **The gotcha:** **no anti-seize** on the plated threads, and don't over-torque. 23 Nm is low.
  Work only on a cold engine (aluminum head).

## Watch out for
- **Preventive coil replacement** sold with plugs: optional on a stock car. Usually an upsell.
- Plugs are a **maintenance item**, so they're not covered by the emissions warranty even though the
  ignition system is emissions-related. If a coil or misfire leads to **catalytic converter** damage,
  the cat itself is covered **8 yr / 80k** federally. See [`../vehicle/warranty-and-coverage.md`](../vehicle/warranty-and-coverage.md).
- Tuned car? Ask for a colder plug/tighter gap per your tuner, not the stock interval.
