# 12V battery (AGM) replacement & registration

**Applies to:** 2019–2024 G20 330i & 330i xDrive · **Category:** maintenance (condition-based)

> **TL;DR / ELI5:** The 12-volt battery starts the car and runs all its electronics. It lives in the
> trunk, under the floor on the right. Modern BMWs are picky: the replacement must be the right
> **type (AGM)** and size, and it has to be **"registered"** with the car's computer so the charging
> system knows it's new. Skip registration and the car keeps charging it like an old, tired battery,
> which shortens its life and causes odd electrical glitches. Batteries usually last 4–8 years
> depending on climate and driving. **Bottom line: replace it when a test shows it's weak (not just
> because of its age), use AGM, and always register it. An independent shop, or a DIY with a $60–150
> app setup, saves hundreds.**

## How much it actually matters: **3/5**
A weak battery causes start-stop shutoffs and module weirdness; a skipped registration shortens the new
battery's life. Not catastrophic, but annoying and avoidable.

## What the forums say
- **Majority view:** **register every new battery**, and **code** the car too if you change the
  amp-hour rating or the type (widely repeated on forums; bimmertalk).
- **Minority view:** "the battery sensor adapts on its own; registration is optional." Not supported
  by a good source. Don't rely on it.
- **Proactive vs run-to-failure:** some replace at 5–6 years because low voltage causes odd module
  faults; others run it to failure and keep a jump pack.
- **Where consensus lands:** AGM out, AGM in, same Ah, **always register**. Replace on a failed test.

## When to do it
| Source | Interval |
|---|---|
| BMW | Condition-based (no fixed interval); battery tested during Vehicle Check |
| Enthusiast / forum consensus | ~4–5 yrs in harsh heat/cold, 6–8 yrs in mild climates; replace on a failed conductance test |
| Signs it's due now | Slow crank, start-stop stops working, "increased battery discharge" / low-voltage messages, odd module faults |

## DIY verdict: **Doable with tools**
- **You need:** an **AGM** battery matching your current label: **70 Ah 61 21 6 805 461** or
  **80 Ah 61 21 7 555 719** (capacity varies by build, so **read your existing battery's label**). Also 10 mm
  (terminals) and 13 mm (hold-down) sockets (⚠️ low-credibility source), and a registration tool:
  BimmerLink + BMW-capable OBD adapter, Carly, or ISTA. Specs:
  [`../vehicle/specs-and-fluids.md`](../vehicle/specs-and-fluids.md).
- **The gotchas:**
  1. **Register** the new battery after install. **Code** too if Ah or type changed.
  2. **Reconnect the vent tube** on the new battery.
  3. When charging or jump-starting, use the **under-hood jump posts**, not the trunk battery terminals,
     so the battery sensor sees the current.
- **Maintainer:** if the car sits 2–3+ weeks, use a smart maintainer on the under-hood posts. Disconnecting
  the battery for storage interrupts the time-based CBS counters (BMW booklet).

## Watch out for
- "Your battery is 4 years old, time to replace it": **ask for the conductance-test printout.** AGM
  batteries often last 5–7 years.
- Big-box non-AGM batteries: wrong type for this car.
- Dealer price is mostly markup on a ~$250 part plus a 5-minute registration.
