# Fuel system service, induction cleaning & carbon (walnut) blasting

**Applies to:** 2019–2024 G20 330i & 330i xDrive (B46/B48, **direct injection only**) · **Category:** maintenance

> **TL;DR / ELI5:** This engine sprays fuel **directly into the cylinders**, so fuel never washes over the
> intake valves. Over many miles a little oily carbon can bake onto those valves. Dealers often sell a
> "fuel induction service" or "fuel system cleaning" (a bottle in the tank plus a spray through the intake).
> **Those products can't reach the intake valves on a direct-injection engine** and aren't on BMW's
> schedule. The real fix for heavy carbon, **walnut blasting**, is only worth it if the engine actually has
> symptoms. Bottom line: **decline the induction/fuel service, buy Top Tier gas, and only blast the valves if a
> borescope or symptoms say so** (typically 80–100k+ if ever).

## How much it actually matters: **2/5**
Symptom-driven. The B48 family is generally reported to build less carbon than older BMW direct-injection
engines (N20/N54), and a look through a borescope is cheap before spending on blasting.

## What the forums say
- **"Skip it" camp (strong consensus on the bottles):** dealer "fuel system service" bottles **don't clean intake
  valves on a direct-injection engine** because fuel never touches the valves; at most they clean injectors and
  combustion chambers, which a ~$10 bottle of PEA cleaner plus Top Tier fuel also does *(common view ⚠️
  unverified forum citation)*.
- **"Do it" camp (walnut blasting):** some blast preventively at 60–100k.
- **Where consensus lands:** **skip the induction service**; walnut-blast only for symptoms (cold-start misfires,
  rough idle) or carbon you've actually *seen* through a borescope.

## When to do it
| Source | Interval |
|---|---|
| BMW | **Not scheduled.** The warranty booklet calls fuel additives "recommended, unscheduled maintenance" that is **not required** to keep the emissions warranty |
| Enthusiast / forum consensus | No interval. Borescope if symptomatic; walnut blast for confirmed buildup (often 80–100k+, if ever) |
| Signs it's due now | Cold-start misfires, rough idle, lean/misfire codes that aren't plugs/coils/injectors, measurable power loss |

## DIY verdict: **Leave it to a shop** (blasting) · **Dead simple** (fuel choice)
- **You need (prevention):** **Top Tier** gasoline at the octane in your manual; occasionally a PEA fuel-system
  cleaner if you like.
- **The gotcha (blasting):** it needs a walnut-media blaster, shop vacuum, intake manifold removal, and turning
  the engine so each cylinder's valves are closed in turn. Not a driveway job. **Spraying cleaner through a
  drive-by-wire throttle body can require a throttle re-adaptation** with a scan tool.

## Watch out for
- **Induction service / fuel injector service / "BG 44K", "EPR", "MOA" / engine flush** lines on a quote:
  **upsell. Decline.** Red-flag threshold: **any charge at all** for these as routine maintenance.
- **Warranty angle (14 states):** **fuel injectors, high-pressure fuel pump, throttle body** and **intake
  manifold gasket** are on BMW's California-list emissions warranty, **7 yr / 70k**, in CA, CO, CT, DE, ME, MD,
  MA, NJ, NY, OR, PA, RI, VT, WA. A real fuel-system fault under 70k goes to a BMW dealer **as a warranty
  claim**, not a paid "cleaning." See [`../vehicle/warranty-and-coverage.md`](../vehicle/warranty-and-coverage.md).
- **EVAP purge valve** problems (hard start, rough idle, ticking) may be under a **15 yr / 150k** extended
  warranty (SIB 01 03 24). Check by VIN. Same doc.
