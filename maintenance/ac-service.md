# A/C service, recharge & cabin "deodorizing"

**Applies to:** 2019–2024 G20 330i & 330i xDrive (refrigerant **R-1234yf**) · **Category:** maintenance

> **TL;DR / ELI5:** The A/C is a sealed system. It doesn't "use up" refrigerant, so there's **no scheduled A/C
> service** on this car. If it blows warm, something is leaking or broken, and a shop should find the leak
> before refilling, otherwise you're paying to vent expensive R-1234yf into the air. A musty smell is usually
> fixed by a **new charcoal cabin filter**, not an "evaporator cleaning." Bottom line: **don't buy A/C service
> or deodorizing as maintenance; pay for diagnosis only if cooling is weak or it smells bad after a new cabin
> filter.**

## How much it actually matters: **1/5** as maintenance (**2/5** if cooling is actually weak)
Not a scheduled item. Weak cooling is a repair, not maintenance.

## What the forums say
- **"Upsell" camp (consensus):** A/C "recharge" without a diagnosed leak, and "evaporator cleaning / climate
  system disinfection / deodorizing," are widely called upsells *(common view ⚠️ unverified forum citation)*.
- **"Do it" camp:** only when the system is low (leak found and fixed) or there's a persistent odor after a
  new cabin filter.
- **Where consensus lands:** low refrigerant = a leak to repair. Musty smell = cabin filter first
  ([`cabin-air-filter.md`](cabin-air-filter.md)). Weak airflow after a filter change on a G20 can be the
  recirculation-flap issue in BMW SIB 64 03 21 (see that doc).

## When to do it
| Source | Interval |
|---|---|
| BMW | **Not scheduled** |
| Enthusiast / forum consensus | Only for weak cooling (after leak diagnosis) or a persistent odor |
| Signs it's due now | Vents blow warm or barely cool; compressor cycling oddly; oily residue at A/C fittings; musty smell that a new cabin filter doesn't fix |

## DIY verdict: **Leave it to a shop** (refrigerant) · **Dead simple** (cabin filter)
- **You need:** nothing for refrigerant work. Shop only. For odor: a new **activated-charcoal cabin filter**.
- **The gotcha:** a "recharge" on a leaking system lasts weeks. Ask for the leak test result *before* the recharge.

## Watch out for
- **Recharge with no leak diagnosis** = red flag.
- **"Evaporator cleaning," "A/C deodorizing," "climate system disinfection"** on a routine visit → decline unless
  there's a smell a new filter didn't fix. Red-flag threshold: **any charge** for these as routine maintenance.
- R-1234yf refrigerant (option `8R9`) is far more expensive than older R-134a, so leaks are worth fixing properly.
