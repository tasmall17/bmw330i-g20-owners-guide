# Coolant & cooling system

**Applies to:** 2019–2024 G20 330i & 330i xDrive (B46/B48) · **Category:** maintenance

> **TL;DR / ELI5:** Coolant (antifreeze) carries heat from the engine to the radiator and keeps it from
> freezing or boiling. BMW fills it with **HT-12** and calls it **"lifetime,"** so there's no scheduled
> flush. The real risk on these engines isn't old coolant, it's **leaks**: plastic hoses, the oil filter
> housing's coolant passage, the thermostat housing. A low-coolant warning you ignore can turn a $300 hose
> into a $5,000 engine. Bottom line: **don't pay for a routine coolant flush**; *do* look for seeps at every
> oil change and fix them immediately. Replace coolant around 5 years / 100k or whenever the system is opened
> for a repair.

## How much it actually matters: flush **2/5** · ignoring a leak **4/5**
The fluid itself lasts a long time. A leak driven on with a low-coolant warning is how a cheap repair becomes
a head-gasket job.

## What the forums say
- **"Skip the flush" camp:** modern BMW coolant lasts 100k+; change only when the system is opened for a repair
  *(common view ⚠️ unverified forum citation)*. Also BMW's position ("long-term rated").
- **"Do it" camp:** many indies treat it as a **4–5 yr / 60k** service because corrosion inhibitors deplete
  (bimmertalk, a low-credibility source).
- **Where consensus lands:** **inspect at every service**; change at ~5 yr / 100k or during any cooling repair;
  **fix seeps now.** Common failures from ~60k: plastic thermostat housing, coolant hoses/quick-connects,
  expansion-tank vent hose, pumps (Euro Premium Parts).

## When to do it
| Source | Interval |
|---|---|
| BMW | **Lifetime / "long-term rated"**: replaced only during repairs |
| Enthusiast / forum consensus | ~5 yr / 100k, or any time the cooling system is opened; some indies say 4–5 yr / 60k |
| Signs it's due now (really: signs of a leak) | Sweet coolant smell, **low-coolant warning**, crusty white/green residue near the oil filter housing, thermostat housing or hoses, temp-gauge swings |

## DIY verdict: **Doable with tools**
- **You need:** **BMW HT-12** (green), **83 19 2 468 442** (1 gal concentrate), mixed **50/50** with distilled
  water; a drain pan; ideally a vacuum fill tool.
- **The gotcha:** the **bleed procedure.** BMW's electric-pump routine is commonly described as: ignition on,
  engine off, heater on max, hold the accelerator ~10 s to start an automatic bleed cycle of 10+ minutes; then
  re-bleed after the first heat cycle ⚠️ unverified (variants exist). **Never open the cap hot.** Don't mix HT-12
  with old blue coolant without a full flush.
- **Which pump?** The B46/B48 is generally believed to use a **belt-driven mechanical main coolant pump plus a
  small electric auxiliary pump** (unlike the older N20's all-electric pump), but some sources say "electric
  water pump." ⚠️ Sources conflict: **confirm for your VIN on RealOEM** before buying parts. If it's
  belt-driven, the drive belt matters more ([`drive-belt.md`](drive-belt.md)).

## Watch out for
- **Warranty angle (14 states):** on the B46/B48 the thermostat lives inside the **heat management module**,
  which is on BMW's **California-list emissions warranty, 7 yr / 70k**, in CA, CO, CT, DE, ME, MD, MA, NJ, NY,
  OR, PA, RI, VT, WA. Thermostat problems under 70k → BMW dealer, as a warranty claim. See
  [`../vehicle/warranty-and-coverage.md`](../vehicle/warranty-and-coverage.md).
- **Coolant leaking from the oil filter housing** is a known issue with its own BMW bulletin (SIB 11 10 25).
  See [`../known-issues/`](../known-issues/README.md).
- A **routine coolant flush at 4–5 years on a healthy car** is mostly an upsell (BMW says lifetime).
- **"Top off" isn't a fix.** If it keeps needing coolant, there's a leak.
