# Engine air filter

**Applies to:** 2019–2024 G20 330i & 330i xDrive (B46/B48) · **Category:** maintenance

> **TL;DR / ELI5:** The engine breathes through a paper filter that catches dust and grit. It lasts a
> long time: BMW calls for a new one about every 40,000 miles, sooner on dusty roads. A clogged filter
> costs a bit of power and efficiency; a torn or missing one lets grit into the turbo. It's one of the
> easiest jobs on the car. **Bottom line: replace it when the car asks or when it looks dirty, and do
> it yourself in about 10 minutes for about $30.**

## How much it actually matters: **2/5**
Only a badly clogged or damaged filter costs anything noticeable.

## What the forums say
- **Little debate.** CBS triggers it; owners just do it.
- **"Sooner" camp:** enthusiast sites suggest checking or replacing around **30k**, sooner in dust
  (bimmertalk, low-credibility source).
- **Oiled "performance" filters:** generally seen as pointless on a stock B48, and can foul the
  airflow sensor (⚠️ unverified general view).
- **Where consensus lands:** inspect at oil changes; replace when dirty or when CBS asks.

## When to do it
| Source | Interval |
|---|---|
| BMW (CBS / 2021 booklet) | Every **4th oil service** (~40k mi), sooner in dusty conditions |
| Enthusiast / forum consensus | Inspect every oil change; replace ~30–40k or when visibly dirty |
| Signs it's due now | CBS shows it due; filter visibly gray/clogged; torn media |

## DIY verdict: **Dead simple**
- **You need:** filter **13 71 8 580 428** (also listed as 13 71 5 A78 8D9), a Torx driver set.
  Specs: [`../vehicle/specs-and-fluids.md`](../vehicle/specs-and-fluids.md).
- **Steps:** open the airbox lid (Torx screws and/or clips), lift the old element out, wipe debris
  from the airbox, drop the new one in, close the lid.
- **The gotcha:** make sure the filter's seal is seated all the way around before closing the lid.
  An unsealed edge lets unfiltered air in.

## Watch out for
- A dealer selling a new air filter **every** visit. Ask to see the old one.
- "Performance" oiled filters: no benefit on a stock engine, and possible sensor fouling.
