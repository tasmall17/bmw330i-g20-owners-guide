# Engine mounts

**Applies to:** G20 330i & 330i xDrive (B46D/B48) · **Category:** known issue

> ⚠️ **Thin research.** This doc was written from a real-world dealer quote plus general BMW
> knowledge. G20-specific forum data, OEM part numbers and independent-shop prices haven't been
> verified yet. Treat the numbers as a starting point and see "To research" below.

> **TL;DR / ELI5:** Engine mounts are the rubber blocks (on these cars believed to be
> **fluid-filled / hydraulic** ⚠️ verify) that hold the engine to the body and soak up vibration.
> As they age the rubber cracks and the fluid can leak out. Then you feel more vibration at idle, or
> a clunk when shifting or pulling away. **Before you pay roughly $2,000 at a dealer, make sure the
> wetness really is mount fluid and not engine oil dripping down from above** (valve cover or oil
> filter housing). Those are different repairs, and oil leaks from parts like the valve cover can
> fall under the emissions warranty.

## How much it actually matters: **3/5**
A failing mount isn't an emergency. It mostly costs comfort (vibration, clunks) and can slowly
add stress to nearby parts and hoses. Fix it when symptoms are clear, but don't let urgency
pressure you into skipping the diagnosis step.

## What the forums say
- ⚠️ **Not yet researched** for the G20. The usual BMW-wide pattern: mounts are a mid-to-high
  mileage wear item, and many owners only notice once the new ones are in ("it feels new again").
- **"Replace in pairs"** is a common BMW and dealer recommendation (mounts age together, and
  balanced stiffness gives smoother running). Some DIYers replace only the failed side when the
  other one tests fine.
- **Where consensus lands (general BMW):** replace when there's a clear failure (crack, leak,
  vibration, clunk). Pairs are reasonable when the car is already apart.

## When it shows up / when to act
| Source | Guidance |
|---|---|
| BMW position | No interval. Replace on failure. Dealers commonly recommend replacing **both** |
| Enthusiast view | ⚠️ not yet researched for the G20 |
| Signs it's happening now | Visible cracks in the mount rubber; fluid wetness *on the mount itself*; more vibration at idle (especially in Drive at a stop); a clunk on shifting into Drive/Reverse or on hard launches; the engine visibly rocks when someone blips the throttle |

## DIY verdict: **Leave it to a shop**
- **You need:** both mounts, new single-use Torx bolts/screws (the quote listed several), an
  engine support bar or jack, and torque specs from BMW repair info.
- **The gotcha:** The engine has to be supported safely while a mount is out, and access around
  the engine support arms is tight.

## Watch out for
- **Diagnose the source of the wetness.** Oil from the
  [valve cover](valve-cover-gasket-and-pcv.md) or the [oil filter housing](oil-filter-housing-leaks.md)
  can run down onto the mount or its support arm and look like the mount is leaking. Ask the shop:
  *"Is that fluid coming out of the mount, or engine oil from above? Can you show me in photos or
  video?"* If it's oil from above, fix the source first. Under 7 yr / 70k in a California-list
  state, the valve cover may be **free** (see
  [`../vehicle/warranty-and-coverage.md`](../vehicle/warranty-and-coverage.md)).
- **Get a second opinion** at an independent BMW shop before approving about $2,000. Labor is
  most of the bill.
- Engine mounts are **not** on the emissions warranty lists.
