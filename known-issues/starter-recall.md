# Starter overheating / fire risk (recall 26V056000)

**Applies to:** 2021–2024 3 Series **330i / 330i xDrive built Nov 26 2020 – May 7 2024** with a
specific starter configuration (~46,384 cars). Expansion 26V438000 covers 3 Series built Oct 10
2022 – Jul 24 2024 · **Category:** known issue (recall)

> **TL;DR / ELI5:** On some cars the engine starter (the electric motor that cranks the engine)
> wears inside its relay chamber after many starts. That leaves metal debris, which can cause a
> **no-start** and, rarely, a **fire** while starting or driving. It's a federal safety recall, so
> the fix is a **free, redesigned starter** at any BMW dealer, with no expiry and no matter who owns
> the car. **Check your VIN today** at nhtsa.gov/recalls or with any BMW dealer. If you already paid
> for a starter, ask BMW about **reimbursement**.

## How much it actually matters: **5/5**
Fire risk, and the fix is free. Do it.

## What the forums say
- Not a debate. Everyone agrees: check the VIN and get it done.
- BMW reviewed four thermal incidents (two in MY2022 3 Series). No accidents or injuries were
  reported. **There's no "park outside" advisory for 26V056**, unlike the related **25V636000**
  (2019–2021 330i starter relay), which does advise parking outside until fixed.

| Enthusiast view | Book it now; combine with your next service visit |
| Signs it's happening now | Intermittent no-start or slow crank, a starter fault code (e.g. pinion timeout), burning smell at startup |

## DIY verdict: **Leave it to a shop** (BMW dealer, free)
- **You need:** your VIN and an appointment.
- **The gotcha:** None, apart from booking the time. It's a long job, so plan transport.

## Watch out for
- **Recalls are always free**, even out of warranty and for second owners. Never pay for a
  recalled part.
- At any visit, ask: *"Any open recalls **or campaigns** on this VIN?"*
- Full recall list and how to check: [`../recalls/README.md`](../recalls/README.md).
